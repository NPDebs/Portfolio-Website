Nurses are essential to maintaining efficient healthcare systems, but the demand for their services continues to outpace supply. The World Health Organization predicts a global shortfall of 12.9 million nurses by 2035\. In Nigeria, factors such as poor healthcare policies, brain drain, and limited nursing school capacity exacerbate the issue. These conditions lead to burnout and job dissatisfaction among the remaining workforce, further weakening the healthcare system. Telehealth has emerged as a potential solution to this crisis by offering healthcare services remotely through digital technology. Telehealth applications, including video conferencing, remote patient monitoring, and mobile health apps, enable healthcare workers to provide care without the need for physical proximity. In Nigeria, telehealth initiatives have already shown promise, particularly in maternal and child health, diabetes care, and mental health services. By reducing the need for travel and in-person consultations, telehealth can alleviate the strain on nurses, mitigate burnout, and extend healthcare access to underserved areas. This paper explores the current nursing shortage in Nigeria, the role telehealth can play in addressing this shortage, and the challenges and benefits of integrating telehealth into the Nigerian healthcare system. Recommendations for policy changes, investments in technology, and training for healthcare workers are proposed as strategies for mitigating the nursing shortage and strengthening healthcare delivery in Nigeria. # **CHAPTER ONE** ## **INTRODUCTION** ## **BACKGROUND** Nurses have been quite accurately referred to as the “backbone of healthcare systems” (Poghosyan & Maier, 2022). Formally trained nurses comprise the largest professional group in many countries and make up roughly half the global healthcare workforce (Drennan & Ross, 2019). Beyond delivering exceptional care, these professionals streamline the delivery of health services and maintain a synergistic workflow between all facets of healthcare provision. Nurses are profoundly valued professionals who contribute significantly to the efficiency of global healthcare systems. An adequate supply of nurses is necessary for ensuring access to quality care, and for this reason, the decline in the number of nurses is a cause for alarm. A stark warning from the World Health Organization (WHO) in 2021 highlighted a looming global shortage of healthcare workers. Their report estimates a current shortfall of 7.2 million, with the demand for nurses alone projected to surge to a staggering 12.9 million by 2035 (Adams et al., 2021). Already in the United Kingdom, a study by Adams et al (2021) reported that over 41,000 nursing posts are vacant across the country's National Health Service (NHS), and this is worsened by the fact that there are more people leaving the profession than joining it. The Nigerian healthcare system is not exempted but also faces this significant challenge: a critical shortage of nurses. The reasons behind this decline are complex. The problem results from the cumulative and compounding effects of factors such as poor policies, limited nursing school capacity, brain drain to developed countries, and unfavourable working conditions (Heijden et al., 2019). These factors create a vicious cycle, where the overworked environment discourages new graduates from entering the field, further exacerbating the shortage. A deficit of nursing staff not only obstructs access to quality care but also strains current nurses, resulting in burnout and decreased job satisfaction. This seminar explores telehealth as a promising solution to bridge this gap and revolutionise nursing practice in Nigeria. Ultimately, the goal of this study is to lay the foundation for understanding how telehealth can potentially revolutionise nursing practice. Telehealth, defined by the World Health Organization as the delivery of healthcare services using information and communication technologies where distance is a barrier, presents a potential but underutilised solution in Nigeria. The seminar is relevant and timely, and can contribute to building a more resilient and efficient healthcare system in Nigeria — one that can effectively address the growing needs of the population. ## **PROBLEM STATEMENT** Despite global advancements in healthcare, the nursing shortage in Nigeria remains a critical issue, which threatens the quality and accessibility of healthcare services. Existing measures have failed to adequately address this gap, leaving many regions, particularly rural areas, underserved. To investigate the key factors contributing to the nursing shortage in Nigeria. 4. To evaluate the potential of telehealth in mitigating the effects of the nursing shortage in Nigeria. ## **CURRENT APPLICATIONS OF TELEHEALTH IN HEALTHCARE** With roots dating back to the 1950s, telemedicine has become a dynamic force shaping healthcare delivery on a global scale (Stoltzfus et Al., 2023). Etymologically, the word is derived from a Greek root word "tele" which means "distance.” The World Health Organization (WHO) defines telemedicine as "The delivery of health care services, where distance is a critical factor, by health care professionals using information and communications technologies for the exchange of valid information for diagnosis, treatment and prevention of disease and injuries, research and evaluation, and for the continuing education of health care providers, all in the interest of advancing the health of individuals and their communities (WHO, 1997).” Telehealth can simply be described as the delivery of healthcare services through the use of information and communication technologies. It encompasses a range of technologies, including communication through networks (video conferencing), remote patient monitoring systems, artificial intelligence techniques, wearable sensors, mobile health applications, cloud computing, smartphones, and hardware tools (Albahri et Al., 2021). Healthcare delivery is fundamentally changing thanks to the seamless connection between information systems and automated tools. Telehealth has gained significant traction in recent years as a means to address healthcare challenges, including workforce shortages. Regular hospital visits can be expensive, particularly in rural areas, due to travel costs (Al Baalharith et Al., 2022). Today, telemedicine is expanding its application in many countries, driven by a strong need to reduce healthcare costs (Stoltzfus et Al., 2023). Globally, as a result of the Covid-19 Pandemic, where physical interaction became risky, people have begun to embrace telemedicine (Imlach et Al., 2020). The pandemic forced the healthcare system to rapidly adapt. As reported by Ezeonwumelu et Al (2022), health workers needed to adopt strategies in the healthcare sector that sought to minimise one-on-one appointments, triage hospital visits, and choose teleconsultations whenever possible, in order to curb the spread of the virus. Fortunately, the use of telehealth services (via video conferencing or other virtual technologies) have reduced medical visits (Garfan et Al., 2021). Thus, it saves time, both on the part of the patient and the health care provider, as well as lightens the financial burden of treatment. Furthermore, Haider et Al. (2022) reported that technology has revolutionised nursing practice by decreasing human error, improving the accessibility of client records, and decreasing burnout related to nursing shortages. Telehealth also contributes to improved patient satisfaction by reducing delayed or cancelled appointments, referral time needed to commute, cost of transportation and time needed to be off work (Haider et Al., 2022). A study by Haleem, et Al. (2021) identified seventeen (17) significant applications of telemedicine in Healthcare. This is summarised below: 1. **Expansion of telehealth access:** Technological advancements have broadened applications in healthcare, enabling remote consultations and accurate diagnostics. 2. **Improved accessibility for disabled patients:** Facilitates access to healthcare services for disabled individuals, reducing travel time and costs. 3. **Efficient remote treatment solutions:** Enables specialised care without long-distance travel, fostering collaboration among healthcare providers. 4. **Treatment for school-going children:** Virtual consultations and on-call medical assistance support, reducing unnecessary visits to urgent care facilities. 5. **Remote treatment for disorders without lab examination:** Offers remote treatment options for conditions not requiring lab examinations, enhancing accessibility during situations like the COVID-19 pandemic. 6. **Virtual doctor appointments:** Enhances healthcare accessibility through virtual doctor appointments, improving medication management and patient quality of life. 7. **Enhanced healthcare system performance:** Technologies improve overall healthcare system performance, offering online healthcare services and remote non-clinical support. 8. **Improved patient coordination:** Improves patient coordination and healthcare service delivery, addressing treatment shortages and overuse of medical care. 9. **Minimised patient travel:** Minimises patient travel time and costs, enabling online appointments and emergency medical attention from the safety of patients' homes. 10. **Teledentistry advancements:** Enhances dental care through remote consultations and specialist collaborations, benefiting remote or underserved populations. 11. **Better service for clinicians and patients:** Supports better service for clinicians and patients, addressing healthcare provider shortages and improving patient care. 12. **Digital health monitoring innovations:** Systems with digital health monitoring capabilities enable accurate diagnoses and treatment recommendations, ensuring safe and efficient medical services. 13. **Skincare consultations:** Dermatologists evaluate and treat patients remotely using high-resolution photographs and film, particularly beneficial for homebound patients. 14. **Patient medication tracking:** Tracks patient medication adherence and health metrics remotely, enhancing healthcare monitoring and management. 15. **Securing patients from infectious diseases:** Secures patients from infectious diseases by enabling remote consultations and robust network security for patient information. 16. **Cost-effective healthcare delivery:** Offers cost-effective healthcare delivery by eliminating the need for travel and reducing consultation expenses. 17. **Reduced need for face-to-face meetings:** Reduces the need for face-to-face meetings, enabling efficient patient monitoring and streamlined emergency care. While telemedicine offers clear advantages and flourishes in developed nations, Nigeria has failed to fully harness this healthcare tool (Egbewande et Al., 2023). In the country, the adoption of telehealth has been gradual, but there are several noteworthy initiatives and applications within the nursing and healthcare practice. One of the prominent applications of telehealth in Nigeria is in the area of maternal and child health. **The Mobile Alliance for Maternal Action** (MAMA) project, launched in 2011, utilises mobile technology to deliver vital health information to pregnant women and new mothers in various Nigerian languages (Jennings et al., 2019). This initiative has been instrumental in providing access to essential healthcare information and support, particularly in rural and underserved communities where access to healthcare facilities is limited. Another area where telehealth has been leveraged is in the management of chronic conditions, such as diabetes and hypertension. **The Diabetic Retinopathy Screening on Wheels** project, implemented in Lagos, uses mobile technology to screen and monitor patients with diabetes, enabling early detection and management of diabetic retinopathy (Adepoju et al., 2021). Here, nurses employ telehealth modalities in providing quality patient care. Furthermore, telehealth has been utilised in the context of mental health care in Nigeria. The COVID-19 pandemic has accelerated the adoption of telepsychiatry and telepsychology services, enabling remote consultations and support for individuals with mental health conditions (Oduguwa et al., 2021). Nurses have been actively involved in these initiatives, providing counselling, follow-up care, and monitoring of patients' mental health status through virtual platforms. The use of mHealth (mobile health) is also gaining popularity. This technology empowers both healthcare professionals and the general public in Nigeria (Chika et Al,. 2024). Instances of mHealth include using mobile phone applications in consultation, monitoring vitals like blood sugar levels, oxygen levels, blood pressure, and more. Nigeria already has different mHealth applications, such as Mobidoc and Kangpe, that allow clients to consult with experienced practitioners directly from their phones. These are but a few examples, and they serve as merely a glimpse into the transformative potential of telehealth in Nigerian nursing practice. The applications of telehealth in nursing practice are constantly evolving. As the technology becomes more accessible and integrated into the healthcare system, we can expect to see even more innovative ways to deliver care remotely. ## **THE IMPACT OF THE NURSING SHORTAGE ON THE NIGERIAN HEALTHCARE SYSTEM** In 2022, the WHO Regional Office for Africa in 2022 conducted a cross-sectional survey of 47 countries in the African Region. This study reported that “the total stock of health workers was approximately 3.6 million across 47 countries (Ahmat et al., 2022\).” Nurses and midwives made up the largest group (37%), followed by community health workers (14%), and doctors (9%) with other healthcare professionals making up \~24%. Results show uneven distribution of health workers within the African Region. Most health workers (85%) are in the public sector. Regional density of physicians, nurses and midwives per 1000 population was 1.55, only 4 countries had densities of more than 4.45 physicians, nurses and midwives per 1000 population. This indicates that while there have been global efforts to strengthen the health workforce (HWF), many countries in Africa still face serious challenges in this area. The nursing shortage in Nigeria has had a significant impact on the healthcare system, affecting both the quality and accessibility of care. According to a report by the World Health Organization (WHO), Nigeria had a ratio of 1.7 nurses and midwives per 1,000 population in 2018, which is significantly below the recommended minimum threshold of 4.45 per 1,000 population (WHO, 2020). This shortage has contributed to increased workloads for existing nurses, leading to burnout, job dissatisfaction, and a higher risk of medical errors (Aliyu et al., 2017). The nursing shortage has also exacerbated the challenges faced by Nigeria's already overburdened healthcare system. With fewer nurses available, healthcare facilities struggle to provide adequate care, resulting in longer wait times, delayed treatments, and compromised patient outcomes (Adeloye et al., 2017). Additionally, the shortage has disproportionately affected rural and underserved areas, where access to healthcare is already limited, further exacerbating health disparities (Ogun et al., 2019). Furthermore, the nursing shortage has had far-reaching economic implications for Nigeria. A study by Kirigia et al. (2019) estimated that the economic cost of the nursing shortage in Nigeria, including productivity losses due to premature mortality and disability, amounted to $2.16 billion in 2016, representing a significant burden on the country's healthcare system and economy. Worthy of note is a study by Aluko et. al (2019), which observed a disappointing trend in maternal and neonatal health indicators, despite efforts like "Health for All" and the Millennium Development Goals. It was reported that several factors, including staff shortages, an imbalance of skilled and semi-skilled personnel, a lack of proper recruitment processes, and inappropriate birthing practices, result in a thorough disservice in the delivery of maternity care in health centres (Aluko et al., 2019). These shortcomings suggest that women are not receiving the high-quality maternal and neonatal care they deserve, further buttressing the point of the concerning impact of nursing decline. ## **FACTORS CONTRIBUTING TO THE NURSING SHORTAGE IN NIGERIA** Despite global recognition of the nursing profession's critical role in healthcare delivery, a significant nursing workforce shortage poses a major threat (Adams et al., 2021). This lack of nurses directly hinders the quality of healthcare services, jeopardising global well-being and progress towards universal health coverage (Yahyaei et al., 2022). Thus, it is imperative to examine the factors that contribute to the nursing shortage, both globally and in Nigeria. Building on the research by Heijden et al. (2019), the nursing shortage in developed countries, including those in the European Union, is expected to worsen over the next two decades. The study identified that this impending crisis stems from an imbalance between the supply of nurses and the ever-growing demand for their services. A systematic review by Tamata et al. (2023) uncovered several factors contributing to the nursing shortage in Nigeria. One of the most prominent is **nurses' stress and burnout**. Overworked nurses experience increased stress, burnout, and even psychosomatic disorders like back and shoulder pain, anger and worry (Adams et al., 2021; Heijden et al., 2019). This strain spills over to their personal lives, affecting their families and social relationships (Hung et al., 2020). Ultimately, these consequences impact patient care, as exhausted nurses may struggle to detect complications early, provide sufficient care, and ensure patient safety (Heijden et al., 2019). Similarly, **poor salaries or no changes to salary** exacerbate the nursing shortage. A key factor fueling the nursing shortage is the issue of stagnant or insufficient salaries. Competitive wages are essential for attracting and retaining talented individuals in any profession. When nurses feel underpaid, especially considering the demanding nature of the work, the long hours, and the emotional strain, they are more likely to seek alternative careers that offer better compensation (Tamata et al., 2023). This inevitably leads to a situation where understaffing creates an even heavier workload for remaining nurses, further perpetuating dissatisfaction and potentially driving even more qualified professionals away. Investing in competitive salaries for nurses is not just financially sound. It is an investment in the well-being of both nurses and the patients they care for. In addition to low salaries, **unfavourable working conditions** further drive nurses away from the profession. Many nurses deal with long shifts, heavy workloads, and limited opportunities for breaks. Studies have identified challenges faced by nurses globally and in Nigeria, including chronic understaffing and a lack of resources (Drennan & Ross, 2019; Heijden et al., 2019). Furthermore, a lack of control over scheduling, limited support from management, and exposure to violence or workplace hazards can further erode morale and job satisfaction. These unfavourable conditions not only push nurses to leave the profession but also make it less appealing to potential new recruits (Alreshidi et al., 2021; Marufu et al., 2021). Likewise, **brain drain** continues to cripple the Nigerian healthcare system. The nursing shortage in Nigeria is terribly exacerbated by the recent "**japa syndrome**," a trend of mass migration amongst highly skilled professionals seeking better opportunities abroad (Okunade & Awosusi, 2023). The allure of higher wages, improved working conditions, and better career prospects abroad creates a powerful incentive for nurses to leave Nigeria, further crippling the domestic healthcare system. Research suggests that this concerning trend of Nigerian nurses migrating to developed countries with better working conditions and higher salaries, has resulted in the persistent shortage of qualified personnel available to handle a growing patient population. Equally concerning is the **limited capacity in nursing schools.** Studies revealed that decreasing nurse enrolment and lack of training for new nursing intakes are the main barriers that affect nursing shortage. Some training barriers are not enough spaces for training, special classrooms, dormitories and clinical sites for practice (LeVasseur & Qureshi, 2019). importing nurses from other countries rather than training local nurses also has significant effects on the nursing workforce. In Nigeria, reports indicate a disparity between the number of qualified applicants and available spots in Nigerian nursing programs. This restricted intake ultimately translates to a smaller pool of nurses entering the workforce. Limited access to nursing education and insufficient training facilities hinder the development of a robust nursing workforce. Moreover, **inadequate human resource and workforce planning** exacerbate the shortage. Inadequate health policies and poor workforce planning contribute significantly to the nursing shortage (Aluko et al., 2019). Many countries lack effective strategies for recruiting and retaining nurses, leading to workforce imbalances (Alreshidi et al., 2021). These barriers in policy and planning lead to nurses receiving poor salary, with little to no incentives. Additionally, **low prospects for professional growth** also discourage both potential and current nurses. The shortage is not just about immediate pressures; it is also about long-term career prospects. A significant factor deterring potential nurses and causing existing ones to leave is the perception of limited opportunities for professional growth (Alreshidi et al., 2021). Many nurses feel their skills stagnate after a certain point, with limited opportunities for specialisation, leadership roles, or advancement within the healthcare system. This lack of a clear career path can be incredibly demotivating, especially for ambitious individuals. Investing in mentorship programs, leadership training opportunities, and well-defined career progression options within nursing can make the profession a more attractive proposition not just for those entering the field, but also for those seeking to build a fulfilling long-term career. Finally, **sociocultural factors** also play a role in the shortage. Sacgaca et al (2024) revealed that sociocultural norms and perceptions can also influence the nursing shortage. In some communities, nursing is perceived as a less prestigious profession, discouraging young people from pursuing careers in this field. Furthermore, **poor policies, regulations and strategies** can also significantly contribute to the nursing shortage. This may manifest as bureaucratic burdens that consume valuable time nurses could spend on patient care. Imagine nurses spending hours filling out excessive paperwork due to outdated regulations, instead of focusing on their patients' needs. Finally, **policies that limit nurses' autonomy in decision-making** can be demotivating. When nurses feel their expertise is undervalued and they lack control over aspects of patient care, it can lead to frustration and a sense of professional disempowerment. Furthermore, policies that fail to address workplace violence or offer inadequate support for nurses dealing with mental health challenges can further exacerbate feelings of burnout and dissatisfaction. ## **THE POTENTIAL OF TELEHEALTH IN MITIGATING THE EFFECTS OF THE NURSING SHORTAGE IN NIGERIA:** ## With the progressively worsening decline in nursing staff, the future of the Nigerian healthcare system appears bleak. However, there is a world of benefits yet to be explored in telehealth. It might indeed be the vehicle that conveys the average man into a healthier tomorrow. Telehealth emerges offers a potential solution to bridge the critical gap in many, few of which are discussed in the paragraphs that follow. One of the most significant advantages of telehealth is its ability to **increase access to care**. A key advantage of telehealth is its ability to extend the reach of nursing care beyond traditional healthcare facilities. For instance, imagine a patient in a remote village, struggling with a chronic illness. Traditionally, accessing specialised care might require a long and arduous journey. Telehealth breaks down this barrier by allowing virtual consultations with nurses. Haleem, et Al (2021) supports this point by stating that “Telemedicine has improved the capacity of healthcare providers to take care of many people without physically being there.” This is made possible through virtual consultations, remote patient monitoring, and mobile health applications. Nurses can now provide care to patients in remote or underserved areas, mitigating the impact of geographic barriers and workforce distribution challenges ( et al., ). This is particularly relevant in a country like Nigeria, where a significant portion of the population resides in rural areas with limited access to healthcare facilities. The approach not only saves patients’ time and resources but also expands their options for receiving essential healthcare services. Additionally, one of the most compelling arguments for telehealth lies in its ability to **mitigate nurse burnout**, a significant factor in workforce attrition. By alleviating some of the workload pressure, telehealth allows nurses to manage their time more effectively. Imagine a nurse conducting a follow-up appointment with a postoperative patient virtually. This eliminates the need for travel and allows the nurse to schedule more consultations throughout the day. Furthermore, telehealth can potentially lead to increased job satisfaction by allowing nurses to focus on tasks that require their expertise and offer more opportunities for patient interaction. Likewise, telehealth has the potential to **promote professional development** among nurses. Continuous education is critical for any profession, and telehealth can support the professional development of nurses. Through online training programs, webinars, and virtual learning platforms, nurses can access educational resources and gain new skills without the need for extensive travel or time away from their workplaces (Okonkwo et al., 2021). This can contribute to the retention and upskilling of the existing nursing workforce, alleviating the impact of the shortage. Moreover, telehealth can also lead to **shorter hospital stays**. With the possibility of effortless remote patient monitoring, patients can be discharged much quicker. This lessens the cost of treatment, while also reducing the risk of contracting nosocomial infections. Moreso, discharged patients can seamlessly transition to recovery at home with the help of telemedicine. Regular check-ins, medication reminders, and even vital sign monitoring can all be conducted virtually, reducing hospital readmission rates and improving overall patient outcomes. In the same vein, telehealth plays a vital role in **streamlining the care delivery process**, enabling more efficient use of nursing resources. By enabling remote consultations and monitoring for certain conditions, telehealth allows nurses to focus their time and expertise on patients with complex needs who require in-person care. Imagine a nurse remotely monitoring a diabetic patient's blood sugar levels through a telemonitoring device. This not only frees up time for the nurse to see patients requiring additional in-person support but also empowers patients to participate actively in their own health management. Lastly, **improving database management** is another area where telehealth can make a significant impact. Electronic Health Records (EHRs), another type of Digital Information Solution, which are important for efficient database management within healthcare systems. By digitising patient records and centralising information, EHRs enable seamless access to patient data, facilitate communication between healthcare providers, and support informed decision-making. Additionally, EHRs enhance data accuracy, security, and interoperability, leading to improved patient outcomes and streamlined administrative processes. ## **SUMMARY** ## There is a critical issue of nursing shortage in Nigeria, exacerbated by factors such as poor policies, brain drain, and limited nursing school capacity. Telehealth offers a promising solution to address this shortage by improving access to healthcare services, especially in underserved areas. Globally, telehealth has been successfully used to reduce healthcare costs and enhance patient care. In Nigeria, telehealth initiatives such as maternal and child health programs and mental health services have shown potential to alleviate the strain on healthcare systems. The nursing shortage has led to increased burnout and job dissatisfaction, which telehealth can help mitigate by reducing the need for in-person consultations and enabling remote care. Furthermore, telehealth supports professional development for nurses through virtual learning platforms and online training programs, helping to upskill the workforce. By streamlining healthcare delivery processes and integrating technologies like remote patient monitoring and electronic health records, telehealth can improve patient outcomes and reduce hospital readmissions. Recommendations include expanding telehealth infrastructure, providing training for healthcare workers, and fostering collaboration among stakeholders to effectively integrate telehealth into the Nigerian healthcare system. ## **CONCLUSION** The nursing shortage is not merely a numbers problem; it is a crisis that strikes at the very heart of healthcare delivery. As nurses leave the profession or migrate to better opportunities, the ripple effects are felt across all levels of patient care, from overburdened hospitals to underserved rural communities. The shortage compromises the quality of care, amplifies health disparities, and increases the strain on an already overwhelmed workforce. This is not a future challenge—it is a present reality that requires immediate attention. Telehealth, with its ability to bridge geographical and workforce gaps, offers a lifeline. But its potential can only be fully realised if we invest in its implementation, train healthcare workers, and shift our mindset toward embracing digital solutions. Finally, there is no wisdom in ignoring the current and projected nursing shortage, as it threatens to birth severe consequences in the healthcare systems globally. Fortunately, telehealth stands as a ray of sunshine, representing a promising solution to fill the void and safeguard our healthcare systems. There is no better time than now, to acknowledge the gravity of the issue and act decisively. It beckons us to ask: Can we offer collective compassion and commitment, or do we let this ticking time bomb implode on us? The answer lies not just in policies and strategies, but in the depths of our collective conscience. The future of healthcare hinges on our ability to act in unison – will we rise to the challenge? ## **RECOMMENDATIONS** Embracing this remedy requires a collaborative effort and strategic initiatives. However, that is only possible when we begin to nurture a culture of genuine concern. To tackle the nursing shortage and improve the quality of healthcare delivery, the following recommendations are proposed: 1. Develop a comprehensive national strategy for integrating telehealth into nursing practice, involving all stakeholders, including policymakers, healthcare providers, educational institutions, and professional organisations. 2. Expand nursing education programs and facilities, with a focus on improving the quality of training and increasing the number of qualified nursing graduates. 3. Implement measures to simplify the nurse licensure process, across different states or regions. This can help address staffing shortages in specific areas. 4. Implement initiatives to improve the working conditions and remuneration of nurses. This will create a supportive and attractive work environment that promotes retention and job satisfaction. 5. Utilise telehealth technologies to facilitate continuous professional development and upskilling for nurses. The goal is to ensure they stay current with the latest practices and technologies. 6. Support nurse-led models of care, by expanding nurse-managed clinics and primary care practices. This allows nurses to utilise their full skillset and potentially reduce reliance on physicians for routine tasks 7. Launch public awareness campaigns and community outreach programs to highlight the critical role of nurses and inspire young people to pursue careers in nursing. 8. Implement technological solutions like electronic health records and automated administrative tasks to free up nurses' time for direct patient care. 9. Create programs to help nurses repay student loans or offer scholarships to ease the financial burden of entering the profession. 10. Promote work-life balance through the provision of flexible work schedules, part-time options, and childcare support programs to accommodate nurses' personal needs. This will significantly reduce burnout and attrition in the workforce. 11. Develop programs to support the mental health and wellbeing of nurses, including access to counselling services, stress management workshops, and peer support groups. 12.
